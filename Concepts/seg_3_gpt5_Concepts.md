# Section: seg_5 Heading：1.1 definitions of statistics, probability, and key terms

- **Statistics**: The science dealing with the collection, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of data.
- **Dot Plot**: A simple graph consisting of a number line with dots positioned above it to display data.
- **Descriptive Statistics**: Organizing and summarizing data, by graphing or using numbers.
- **Inferential Statistics**: Formal methods for drawing conclusions from data.
- **Statistical Inference**: Using probability to determine how confident we can be that conclusions are correct.
- **Probability**: A mathematical tool used to study randomness; it deals with the chance (likelihood) of an event occurring.
- **Population**: A collection of persons, things, or objects under study.
- **Sampling**: Selecting a portion (subset) of a larger population to study to gain information about the population.
- **Sample**: The selected portion (subset) of the population used to study the population.
- **Statistic**: A number that represents a property of the sample; an estimate of a population parameter.
- **Parameter**: A numerical characteristic of the whole population that can be estimated by a statistic.
- **Representative Sample**: A sample that contains the characteristics of the population.
- **Variable**: A characteristic or measurement that can be determined for each member of a population.
- **Numerical Variable**: A variable that takes on values with equal units, such as weight in pounds or time in hours.
- **Categorical Variable**: A variable that places a person or thing into a category.
- **Data**: The actual values of the variable resulting from sampling; they may be numbers or words.
- **Datum**: A single value.
- **Mean**: The arithmetic average of a set of numbers, computed by adding the values and dividing by the number of values.
- **Proportion**: The fraction of the total in a given category (count in category divided by total).

# Section: seg_7 Heading：1.2 data, sampling, and variation in data and sampling

- **Qualitative Data**: Data resulting from categorizing or describing attributes; often described by words or letters.
- **Categorical Data**: Another term for qualitative data.
- **Quantitative Data**: Numerical data resulting from counting or measuring attributes.
- **Quantitative Discrete Data**: Counting-based numerical data that take on only certain values.
- **Quantitative Continuous Data**: Measurement-based numerical data that may include fractions, decimals, or irrational numbers.

- **Pie Chart**: Graph where categories are wedges proportional to category percentages; requires no missing categories and percentages that sum to 100%.
- **Bar Graph**: Graph with bars whose lengths are proportional to the number or percent in each category; bars may be vertical or horizontal.
- **Pareto Chart**: Bar graph with categories sorted from largest to smallest.
- **Relative Frequency**: Percentages or proportions used alongside counts to compare categories.

- **Sample**: A subset used to represent a population and intended to have the same characteristics as the population.
- **Random Sampling**: Sampling in which each member of the population initially has an equal chance of being selected.
- **Simple Random Sample**: Sampling where any group of n individuals is equally likely to be chosen; each sample of the same size has an equal chance of selection.
- **Stratified Sample**: Sampling by dividing the population into groups (strata) and taking a proportionate number from each.
- **Strata**: Groups into which a population is divided for stratified sampling.
- **Cluster Sample**: Sampling by dividing the population into clusters and randomly selecting some clusters, including all members in selected clusters.
- **Systematic Sample**: Sampling by randomly selecting a starting point and taking every nth member from a listing.
- **Convenience Sampling**: Non-random sampling using readily available results; may be biased.

- **Sampling With Replacement**: After selection, a member is returned to the population and may be chosen again.
- **Sampling Without Replacement**: A member may be chosen only once.

- **Sampling Error**: Error caused by the sampling process itself (e.g., insufficient sample size).
- **Nonsampling Error**: Error from factors not related to sampling (e.g., defective counting device).
- **Sampling Bias**: Bias occurring when some population members are not as likely to be chosen as others.

- **Problems With Samples**: Non-representative (biased) samples lead to inaccurate, invalid results.
- **Self-Selected Samples**: Responses only from those who choose to respond; often unreliable.
- **Sample Size Issues**: Samples that are too small may be unreliable; larger samples are better when possible.
- **Undue Influence**: Collecting data or asking questions in ways that influence responses.
- **Non-Response**: Refusal or failure to participate can make responses non-representative.
- **Causality**: A relationship between two variables does not imply one causes the other.
- **Self-Funded Or Self-Interest Studies**: Studies conducted to support a claim; must be evaluated for impartiality.
- **Misleading Use Of Data**: Use of improperly displayed graphs, incomplete data, or lack of context.
- **Confounding**: When effects of multiple factors on a response cannot be separated.

- **Variation In Data**: Natural variability present in any set of data.
- **Variation In Samples**: Random samples from the same population will likely differ from each other.
- **Sample Size**: The number of observations in a sample; an important factor in analysis.

# Section: seg_9 Heading：1.3 frequency, frequency tables, and levels of measurement

- **Level Of Measurement**: The way a set of data is measured; determines which statistical procedures are appropriate.
- **Nominal Scale Level**: Qualitative categories or labels with no inherent order; cannot be used in calculations.
- **Ordinal Scale Level**: Ordered categories where differences between values are not measurable; cannot be used in calculations.
- **Interval Scale Level**: Ordered data with meaningful differences but no true zero starting point; calculations are allowed, but ratio comparisons are not meaningful.
- **Ratio Scale Level**: Ordered data with meaningful differences and a true zero point; ratios and calculations are meaningful.
- **Frequency**: The number of times a data value occurs; the sum of frequencies equals the total number of observations.
- **Relative Frequency**: The ratio of a value’s frequency to the total number of outcomes; expressible as a fraction, percent, or decimal; values sum to 1 (subject to rounding).
- **Cumulative Relative Frequency**: The accumulation of relative frequencies up to a given value, found by adding previous relative frequencies to the current one; the final entry equals 1 (approximately when rounding occurs).
- **Frequency Table**: A table listing distinct data values (often in ascending order) and their frequencies, optionally including relative and cumulative relative frequency columns.
- **Rounding Off Rules**: Round only the final answer, carrying it one more decimal place than the original data; avoid rounding intermediate results, or if necessary, carry at least twice as many decimal places as in the final answer.

# Section: seg_11 Heading：1.4 experimental design and ethics

- **Randomized Experiment**: Researcher manipulates values of the explanatory variable and measures resulting changes in the response variable.
- **Explanatory Variable**: Variable that causes change in another variable.
- **Response Variable**: Variable that is affected by changes in the explanatory variable.
- **Treatments**: Different values of the explanatory variable.
- **Experimental Unit**: Single object or individual to be measured.
- **Lurking Variables**: Additional variables that can cloud a study.
- **Random Assignment**: Assigning experimental units to treatment groups randomly to spread lurking variables equally and isolate the explanatory variable.
- **Control Group**: Treatment group set aside to receive a placebo to balance the effects of being in an experiment with the effects of the active treatments.
- **Placebo**: Treatment that cannot influence the response variable.
- **Blinding**: Condition in which a person involved in the study does not know who is receiving active treatments or a placebo.
- **Double-Blind Experiment**: Experiment in which both the subjects and the researchers involved with the subjects are blinded.
- **Institutional Review Board (IRB)**: Oversight committee established by research institutions to ensure the safety of human subjects; all planned studies must be approved in advance.
- **Risk Minimization**: Requirement that risks to participants be minimized and reasonable relative to projected benefits.
- **Informed Consent**: Process in which risks are clearly explained to participants, written consent is obtained, and documentation is kept.
- **Privacy Protection**: Requirement that data collected from individuals be guarded carefully to protect their privacy.
- **Falsifying Data**: Unethical practice of creating or altering data, changing measuring instruments without reporting, or misrepresenting the number of experimental subjects.

# Section: seg_13 Heading：1.5 data collection experiment

- **Systematic Sampling Technique**: Selecting sample units by starting with a random selection and then selecting at a fixed step through a list until the desired number is reached.
- **Relative Frequency Table**: A table that summarizes data categories with their frequency, relative frequency, and cumulative relative frequency.
- **Frequency**: The number of data values in a category.
- **Relative Frequency**: The percent of the dataset in a category.
- **Cumulative Relative Frequency**: The running total of relative frequencies up to and including a category.
- **Data Grouping**: Organizing data into different category groupings (such as single values or combined intervals) for tabulation and interpretation.

# Section: seg_15 Heading：1.6 sampling experiment

- **Simple Random Sampling**: A straightforward method for selecting a random sample; assign numbers to all population members and use a random number generator to select labels identifying the sample.

- **Systematic Sampling**: A method for selecting a random sample by listing the population, randomly choosing a starting point, computing k = (population size)/(desired sample size), and selecting every kth individual.

- **Stratified Sampling**: A method to ensure adequate representation of subgroups; divide the population into strata and use simple random sampling to select a proportionate number from each stratum.

- **Cluster Sampling**: A method that divides the population into clusters and uses simple random sampling to select clusters; all individuals in selected clusters are included in the sample.

- **Random Sampling**: A method of selection that gives every member of the population an equal chance of being chosen.

- **Sampling With Replacement**: After selection, a member is returned to the population before the next draw.

- **Sampling Without Replacement**: A selected member is not returned to the population and cannot be chosen again.

- **Population**: All individuals, objects, or measurements whose properties are being studied.

- **Sample**: A subset of the population studied.

- **Representative Sample**: A subset of the population that has the same characteristics as the population.

- **Sampling Bias**: A condition in which not all members of the population are equally likely to be selected.

- **Sampling Error**: Natural variation from using a sample to represent a population; decreases as sample size increases.

- **Nonsampling Error**: Reliability issues not due to natural sampling variation, including poor study design, biased methods, inaccurate responses, data entry errors, and poor analysis.
