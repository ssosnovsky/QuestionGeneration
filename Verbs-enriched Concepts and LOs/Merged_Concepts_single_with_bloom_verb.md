# Section: seg_5 Heading：1.1 definitions of statistics, probability, and key terms
- **Statistics** (define, identify, interpret) : The science dealing with the collection, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of data.
- **Descriptive Statistics** (summarize, calculate, construct) : Organizing and summarizing data, by graphing or using numbers.
- **Inferential Statistics** (define, describe, differentiate) : Formal methods for drawing conclusions from data.
- **Statistical Inference** (define, describe, differentiate) : Using probability to determine how confident we can be that conclusions are correct.
- **Probability** (define, describe, recognize) : A mathematical tool used to study randomness; it deals with the chance (likelihood) of an event occurring.
- **Population** (define, identify, differentiate) : A collection of persons, things, or objects under study.
- **Sampling** (define, identify, differentiate) : Selecting a portion (subset) of a larger population to study to gain information about the population.
- **Sample** (define, identify, differentiate) : The selected portion (subset) of the population used to study the population.
- **Statistic** (define, identify, calculate) : A number that represents a property of the sample; an estimate of a population parameter.
- **Parameter** (define, identify, differentiate) : A numerical characteristic of the whole population that can be estimated by a statistic.
- **Representative Sample** (define, recognize) : A sample that contains the characteristics of the population.
- **Variable** (define, identify, classify) : A characteristic or measurement that can be determined for each member of a population.
- **Numerical Variable** (define, classify, differentiate) : A variable that takes on values with equal units, such as weight in pounds or time in hours.
- **Categorical Variable** (define, identify, differentiate) : A variable that places a person or thing into a category.
- **Data** (define, identify, exemplify) : The actual values of the variable resulting from sampling; they may be numbers or words.
- **Mean** (define, calculate, explain) : The arithmetic average of a set of numbers, computed by adding the values and dividing by the number of values.
- **Proportion** (calculate, identify) : The fraction of the total in a given category (count in category divided by total).

# Section: seg_7 Heading：1.2 data, sampling, and variation in data and sampling
- **Qualitative Data** (define, classify, exemplify) : Data resulting from categorizing or describing attributes; often described by words or letters.
- **Categorical Data** (define, identify, describe) : Another term for qualitative data.
- **Quantitative Data** (define, classify, differentiate) : Numerical data resulting from counting or measuring attributes.
- **Quantitative Discrete Data** (define, classify, exemplify) : Counting-based numerical data that take on only certain values.
- **Quantitative Continuous Data** (define, classify, differentiate) : Measurement-based numerical data that may include fractions, decimals, or irrational numbers.
- **Pie Chart** (define, explain, compare) : Graph where categories are wedges proportional to category percentages; requires no missing categories and percentages that sum to 100%.
- **Bar Graph** (define, identify, interpret) : Graph with bars whose lengths are proportional to the number or percent in each category; bars may be vertical or horizontal.
- **Pareto Chart** (define, recognize, differentiate) : Bar graph with categories sorted from largest to smallest.
- **Relative Frequency** (define, compare, interpret) : Percentages or proportions used alongside counts to compare categories.
- **Sample** (define, classify, asses) : A subset used to represent a population and intended to have the same characteristics as the population.
- **Random Sampling** (define, classify, execute) : Sampling in which each member of the population initially has an equal chance of being selected.
- **Simple Random Sample** (define, identify, construct) : Sampling where any group of n individuals is equally likely to be chosen; each sample of the same size has an equal chance of selection.
- **Stratified Sample** (define, classify, construct) : Sampling by dividing the population into groups (strata) and taking a proportionate number from each.
- **Strata** (define, describe, identify) : Groups into which a population is divided for stratified sampling.
- **Cluster Sample** (define, classify, implement) : Sampling by dividing the population into clusters and randomly selecting some clusters, including all members in selected clusters.
- **Systematic Sample** (define, classify, implement) : Sampling by randomly selecting a starting point and taking every nth member from a listing.
- **Convenience Sampling** (define, identify, asses) : Non-random sampling using readily available results; may be biased.
- **Sampling With Replacement** (define, compare, explain) : After selection, a member is returned to the population and may be chosen again.
- **Sampling Without Replacement** (define, compare, calculate) : A member may be chosen only once.
- **Sampling Error** (define, differentiate, identify) : Error caused by the sampling process itself (e.g., insufficient sample size).
- **Nonsampling Error** (define, differentiate, identify) : Error from factors not related to sampling (e.g., defective counting device).
- **Sampling Bias** (define, identify, explain) : Bias occurring when some population members are not as likely to be chosen as others.
- **Problems With Samples** (identify, asses, explain) : Non-representative (biased) samples lead to inaccurate, invalid results.
- **Self-Selected Samples** (define, identify, state) : Responses only from those who choose to respond; often unreliable.
- **Sample Size Issues** (define, explain, identify) : Samples that are too small may be unreliable; larger samples are better when possible.
- **Undue Influence** (define, identify) : Collecting data or asking questions in ways that influence responses.
- **Non-Response** (define, explain, recognize) : Refusal or failure to participate can make responses non-representative.
- **Causality** (define, explain, recognize) : A relationship between two variables does not imply one causes the other.
- **Self-Funded Or Self-Interest Studies** (define, recognize, describe) : Studies conducted to support a claim; must be evaluated for impartiality.
- **Misleading Use Of Data** (identify, explain, asses) : Use of improperly displayed graphs, incomplete data, or lack of context.
- **Confounding** (define, describe) : When effects of multiple factors on a response cannot be separated.
- **Variation In Data** (define, describe, explain) : Natural variability present in any set of data.
- **Variation In Samples** (define, explain, recognize) : Random samples from the same population will likely differ from each other.
- **Sample Size** (define, describe, explain) : The number of observations in a sample; an important factor in analysis.

# Section: seg_9 Heading：1.3 frequency, frequency tables, and levels of measurement
- **Level Of Measurement** (define, list, describe) : The way a set of data is measured; determines which statistical procedures are appropriate.
- **Nominal Scale Level** (define, describe, classify) : Qualitative categories or labels with no inherent order; cannot be used in calculations.
- **Ordinal Scale Level** (define, identify, compare) : Ordered categories where differences between values are not measurable; cannot be used in calculations.
- **Interval Scale Level** (define, compare, exemplify) : Ordered data with meaningful differences but no true zero starting point; calculations are allowed, but ratio comparisons are not meaningful.
- **Ratio Scale Level** (define, compare, classify) : Ordered data with meaningful differences and a true zero point; ratios and calculations are meaningful.
- **Frequency** (define, calculate, construct) : The number of times a data value occurs; the sum of frequencies equals the total number of observations.
- **Relative Frequency** (define, calculate, interpret) : The ratio of a value’s frequency to the total number of outcomes; expressible as a fraction, percent, or decimal; values sum to 1 (subject to rounding).
- **Cumulative Relative Frequency** (define, calculate, interpret) : The accumulation of relative frequencies up to a given value, found by adding previous relative frequencies to the current one; the final entry equals 1 (approximately when rounding occurs).
- **Frequency Table** (construct, calculate, interpret) : A table listing distinct data values (often in ascending order) and their frequencies, optionally including relative and cumulative relative frequency columns.
- **Rounding Off Rules** (state, describe, use) : Round only the final answer, carrying it one more decimal place than the original data; avoid rounding intermediate results, or if necessary, carry at least twice as many decimal places as in the final answer.

# Section: seg_11 Heading：1.4 experimental design and ethics
- **Randomized Experiment** (define, explain, design) : Researcher manipulates values of the explanatory variable and measures resulting changes in the response variable.
- **Explanatory Variable** (define, identify, describe) : Variable that causes change in another variable.
- **Response Variable** (define, identify, describe) : Variable that is affected by changes in the explanatory variable.
- **Treatments** (define, identify, list) : Different values of the explanatory variable.
- **Experimental Unit** (define, identify) : Single object or individual to be measured.
- **Lurking Variables** (define, identify, explain) : Additional variables that can cloud a study.
- **Random Assignment** (define, explain, identify) : Assigning experimental units to treatment groups randomly to spread lurking variables equally and isolate the explanatory variable.
- **Control Group** (define, identify, explain) : Treatment group set aside to receive a placebo to balance the effects of being in an experiment with the effects of the active treatments.
- **Placebo** (define, identify, explain) : Treatment that cannot influence the response variable.
- **Blinding** (define, explain, identify) : Condition in which a person involved in the study does not know who is receiving active treatments or a placebo.
- **Double-Blind Experiment** (define, identify, describe) : Experiment in which both the subjects and the researchers involved with the subjects are blinded.
- **Institutional Review Board (IRB)** (define, describe, state) : Oversight committee established by research institutions to ensure the safety of human subjects; all planned studies must be approved in advance.
- **Risk Minimization** (define, identify) : Requirement that risks to participants be minimized and reasonable relative to projected benefits.
- **Informed Consent** (define, describe) : Process in which risks are clearly explained to participants, written consent is obtained, and documentation is kept.
- **Privacy Protection** (define, explain) : Requirement that data collected from individuals be guarded carefully to protect their privacy.
- **Falsifying Data** (identify, describe, list) : Unethical practice of creating or altering data, changing measuring instruments without reporting, or misrepresenting the number of experimental subjects.

# Section: seg_19 Heading：2.1 stem-and-leaf graphs (stemplots), line graphs, and bar graphs
- **Stem-And-Leaf Graph (Stemplot)** (construct, identify, interpret) : A graph for small data sets that divides each observation into a stem and a leaf (final significant digit), lists stems vertically from smallest to largest, and places leaves in increasing order next to their stems to give an exact picture of the data.
- **Stem** (define, identify, construct) : The portion of each observation in a stemplot that precedes the final significant digit and is listed in a vertical column from smallest to largest.
- **Leaf** (define, identify, construct) : The final significant digit of each observation in a stemplot, written in increasing order next to its corresponding stem.
- **Side-By-Side Stem-And-Leaf Plot** (define, construct, compare) : A comparative stemplot in which two data sets share the same stems, with leaves displayed on both the left and right of the stems.
- **Outlier** (define, identify) : An observation that does not fit the rest of the data, sometimes called an extreme value, appearing not to fit the pattern of the graph.
- **Line Graph** (define, construct, describe) : A graph for specific data values with the x-axis showing data values, the y-axis showing frequency points, and the points connected by line segments.
- **Bar Graph** (construct, describe, define) : A graph consisting of separated bars that can be vertical or horizontal and may be rectangles or rectangular boxes in three-dimensional plots.

# Section: seg_21 Heading：2.2 histograms, frequency polygons, and time series graphs
- **Histogram** (define, construct, calculate) : A graph of contiguous boxes with the horizontal axis labeled by what the data represent and the vertical axis labeled frequency or relative frequency; used to readily display large data sets and show the data’s shape, center, and spread, with the same shape under either y-axis label.
- **Frequency** (define, calculate, identify) : The number of times an answer occurs.
- **Relative Frequency** (define, calculate) : The frequency for an observed value divided by the total number of data values in the sample (RF = f/n).
- **Class Interval (Class)** (define, calculate, construct) : The bars or intervals that represent grouped data in a histogram.
- **Class Width** (define, calculate, construct) : The width of each bar or class interval computed as (ending value − starting point) divided by the number of bars.
- **Starting Point (Histogram)** (define, calculate, explain) : A first class boundary chosen to be less than the smallest data value and carried to one more decimal place than the value with the most decimal places.
- **Class Boundaries Convention** (define, explain, use) : Carry boundaries to one additional decimal place so no data value falls on a boundary; count a value in a class if it falls on the left boundary but not if it falls on the right boundary.
- **Number Of Classes** (calculate, state) : Typically five to 15 bars for clarity; a guideline is to take the square root of the number of data values and round to determine the number of intervals.
- **Continuous Data** (classify, differentiate, identify) : Data obtained by measurement.
- **Discrete Data** (define, identify, classify) : Data obtained by counting.
- **Discrete Data Class Boundaries** (calculate, construct) : For integer data, subtract 0.5 from the smallest value and add 0.5 to the largest value; choose a width that centers data values within intervals.
- **Frequency Polygon** (construct, compare, describe) : A graph analogous to a line graph that makes continuous data easy to interpret, constructed by selecting class intervals, plotting the data points, and connecting them with line segments.
- **Time Series Graph** (construct, define, describe) : A graph that recognizes chronological ordering by plotting time on the horizontal axis and measured values on the vertical axis, connecting points in the order they occur to display changes over time.
- **Uses Of A Time Series Graph** (describe, explain, identify) : Important for data recorded over time because graphical display makes trends easy to spot.

# Section: seg_23 Heading：2.3 measures of the location of the data
- **Measures Of Location** (define, calculate, interpret) : Common measures that describe data position, specifically quartiles and percentiles.
- **Percentiles** (define, calculate, interpret) : Values that divide ordered data into hundredths; the pth percentile has at most p% of data at or below it.
- **Quartiles** (define, calculate, interpret) : Special percentiles that divide ordered data into quarters.
- **First Quartile (Q1)** (define, calculate, interpret) : The 25th percentile; the median of the lower half of ordered data.
- **Median (Second Quartile, 50th Percentile)** (define, calculate, interpret) : The center of ordered data that splits values into two halves; may not be an observed value.
- **Third Quartile (Q3)** (define, calculate, interpret) : The 75th percentile; the median of the upper half of ordered data.
- **Interquartile Range (IQR)** (calculate, compare, detect) : The spread of the middle 50% of data; calculated as Q3 − Q1.
- **Potential Outlier** (define, calculate, identify) : A data point notably different from others; suspected if less than Q1 − 1.5(IQR) or greater than Q3 + 1.5(IQR); requires further investigation.
- **Five-Number Summary** (define, calculate, interpret) : The set of Minimum, Q1, Median, Q3, and Maximum.
- **Kth Percentile Index Formula** (calculate, explain, state) : For ordered data of size n, the position i = (k/100)(n + 1); if i is an integer, use the ith value; otherwise average the surrounding values.
- **Percentile Rank Formula** (calculate, interpret) : For a value with x observations below and y equal in a set of size n, percentile = ((x + 0.5y)/n) × 100, rounded to the nearest integer.
- **Interpreting Percentiles** (interpret, explain, define) : Percentiles indicate relative standing; low percentiles correspond to lower values and high percentiles to higher values; whether a percentile is “good” or “bad” depends on context.
- **Percentile Interpretation Elements** (list, interpret, compose) : Include context, the data value, the percent below the percentile, and the percent above the percentile.

# Section: seg_25 Heading：2.4 box plots
- **Box Plot (Box-And-Whisker Plot)** (construct, interpret, calculate) : A graphical display constructed from five values that shows the concentration of the data and how far extreme values are from most of the data; the box spans Q1 to Q3 with a median line, and whiskers extend to the minimum and maximum.
- **Five Values For Box Plots** (list, calculate, describe) : The minimum value, first quartile, median (second quartile), third quartile, and maximum value used to construct a box plot.
- **Minimum Value** (identify, use, describe) : The smallest data value used as an axis endpoint and whisker limit in a box plot.
- **Maximum Value** (define, identify, use) : The largest data value used as an axis endpoint and whisker limit in a box plot.
- **First Quartile (Q1)** (define, identify, use) : The value that marks one end of the box; approximately 25% of the data lie between the minimum and Q1.
- **Median (Q2)** (define, identify, compute) : The second quartile shown inside the box (often as a dashed line) that may lie between or coincide with the first and third quartiles.
- **Third Quartile (Q3)** (calculate, identify, interpret) : The value that marks the other end of the box; approximately 25% of the data lie between Q3 and the maximum.
- **Whiskers** (define, describe, explain) : Line segments extending from the ends of the box to the smallest and largest data values; when outliers are marked with dots, whiskers do not extend to the minimum and maximum.
- **Interquartile Range (IQR)** (define, calculate, compare) : The difference Q3 − Q1, representing the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
- **Middle 50%** (identify, calculate, compare) : The portion of the data between the first and third quartiles that falls inside the box.
- **Tied Values In Box Plots** (identify, describe, interpret) : A condition where some of the minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile, and maximum are equal, affecting the display (e.g., no separate median line when the median equals the third quartile).
- **Quartile Proportions** (state, describe, interpret) : Each quartile contains approximately 25% of the data.

# Section: seg_27 Heading：2.5 measures of the center of the data
- **Arithmetic Mean (Average)** (calculate, define, compare) : Sum of all data values divided by the number of values; commonly used as a measure of center.
- **Sample Mean (X̄)** (calculate, define, identify) : Arithmetic mean of a sample, denoted x̄; a good estimate of the population mean when the sample is truly random.
- **Population Mean (Μ)** (identify, calculate, differentiate) : Arithmetic mean of an entire population, denoted μ.
- **Mean Calculation Using Frequencies** (calculate, define, describe) : Compute the mean by multiplying each distinct value by its frequency, summing these products, and dividing by the total number of data values.
- **Median** (define, calculate, compare) : The value that splits ordered data into two equal parts; for odd n it is the middle value, and for even n it is the average of the two middle values; robust to outliers.
- **Median Location Formula** (calculate, state, differentiate) : The position of the median in ordered data is (n + 1) / 2; the location is not the median’s value.
- **Mode** (define, calculate, classify) : The most frequent value; multiple modes are allowed if they tie for highest frequency; applicable to qualitative and quantitative data.
- **Frequency Table** (define, calculate, identify) : A representation of grouped data that lists intervals and their corresponding frequencies.
- **Midpoint Of An Interval** (define, calculate, use) : The average of an interval’s lower and upper boundaries, used to represent the interval’s data.
- **Mean Of A Grouped Frequency Table** (calculate, describe) : Estimated mean computed as Σ(fm) / Σf, where f is interval frequency and m is the interval midpoint.

# Section: seg_29 Heading：2.6 skewness and the mean, median, and mode
- **Symmetrical Distribution** (define, recognize, describe) : A distribution in which a vertical line can be drawn so the left and right shapes are mirror images of each other.
- **Skewed To The Left** (define, identify, describe) : A distribution that is pulled out to the left.
- **Skewed To The Right** (define, identify, describe) : A distribution that is pulled out to the right.
- **Mean And Median In Perfectly Symmetrical Distribution** (state, describe) : In a perfectly symmetrical distribution, the mean and the median are the same.
- **Mean Versus Median Sensitivity To Skewness** (describe, compare, summarize) : The mean and the median both reflect skewing, but the mean reflects it more.
- **Relative Order In Left-Skewed Distribution** (state, describe, recognize) : Generally, the mean is less than the median, which is often less than the mode.
- **Relative Order In Right-Skewed Distribution** (state, describe, compare) : Generally, the mode is often less than the median, which is less than the mean.
- **Median And Mean Positions Relative To Mode In Skewed Distributions** (describe, state, compare) : The median is closest to the high point (the mode), while the mean tends to be farther out on the tail.
- **Mean And Median Location In Symmetrical Distribution** (state, describe) : In a symmetrical distribution, the mean and the median are centrally located close to the high point of the distribution.

# Section: seg_31 Heading：2.7 measures of the spread of the data
- **Standard Deviation** (define, calculate, interpret) : A number that measures how far data values are from their mean; a numerical measure of overall variation that is always zero or positive.
- **Deviation** (define, calculate, interpret) : For a value x, the difference from the mean (population: x − μ; sample: x − x̄) used to compute spread.
- **Variance** (define, calculate, differentiate) : The average of the squared deviations (σ² for a population, s² for a sample); the standard deviation is the square root of the variance.
- **Sample Standard Deviation** (define, calculate, differentiate) : The standard deviation calculated from sample data, denoted s, computed with denominator n − 1.
- **Population Standard Deviation** (define, calculate, differentiate) : The standard deviation calculated from an entire population, denoted σ, computed with denominator N.
- **Number Of Standard Deviations (#ofSTDEVs)** (calculate, interpret, compare) : How many standard deviations a value is from the mean; relates to data via value = mean + (#ofSTDEVs)(standard deviation).
- **Z-Score** (calculate, compare, interpret) : The number of standard deviations a value is from its mean; for a sample z = (x − x̄)/s and for a population z = (x − μ)/σ; used to compare values across different data sets.
- **Grouped Data Standard Deviation** (calculate, explain, interpret) : An estimated standard deviation for grouped frequency data obtained using interval midpoints because individual data values are unknown.
- **Variability** (describe, interpret, calculate) : Describing data in terms of its spread.
- **Chebyshev's Rule** (state, compare, identify) : For any data set, at least 75% of values lie within two standard deviations of the mean, at least 89% within three, and at least 95% within 4.5.
- **Empirical Rule** (state, identify, compare) : For bell-shaped, symmetric distributions, approximately 68% of values lie within one standard deviation of the mean, 95% within two, and more than 99% within three; applies only to such distributions.

# Section: seg_33 Heading：2.8 descriptive statistics
- **Box Plot** (construct, interpret, compare) : A graph that gives a quick picture of the middle 50% of the data.
- **First Quartile** (define, calculate, interpret) : The value that is the median of the lower half of the ordered data set.
- **Frequency** (define, explain, use) : The number of times a value of the data occurs.
- **Frequency Table** (define, construct, calculate) : A data representation in which grouped data is displayed along with the corresponding frequencies.
- **Histogram** (construct, describe, interpret) : A graphical representation of a data distribution using contiguous rectangles; x represents data (classes) and y represents frequency or relative frequency.
- **Interquartile Range (IQR)** (define, calculate, use) : The range of the middle 50% of data values, found by subtracting the first quartile (Q1) from the third quartile (Q3).
- **Interval (Class Interval)** (define, construct, use) : A range of data used when displaying large data sets.
- **Mean** (define, calculate, compare) : A measure of central tendency (arithmetic average); sample mean x̄ equals the sum of sample values divided by the sample size, and population mean μ equals the sum of population values divided by the population size.
- **Median** (define, calculate, compare) : A value that separates ordered data into halves; half the values are at or below and half are at or above it.
- **Outlier** (define, identify, calculate) : An observation that does not fit the rest of the data.
- **Percentile** (define, calculate, interpret) : A number that divides ordered data into hundredths; the median is the 50th percentile, Q1 is the 25th, and Q3 is the 75th.
- **Quartiles** (define, calculate, use) : Numbers that separate data into quarters; the second quartile is the median.
- **Relative Frequency** (define, calculate, explain) : The ratio of the frequency of a value to the total number of outcomes.
- **Skewed** (define, classify, describe) : Describes data that is not symmetrical; more spread in lower values indicates left skew, more spread in greater values indicates right skew.
- **Standard Deviation** (calculate, interpret, compare) : The square root of the variance that measures how far data values are from their mean; notation s for sample and σ for population.
- **IQR Outlier Rule** (calculate, identify, use) : A method to flag potential outliers using Q3 + 1.5(IQR) and Q1 − 1.5(IQR).
- **Sample Standard Deviation** (calculate, use, interpret) : s = sqrt[∑(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)] (or frequency form), measuring spread for a sample.
- **Population Standard Deviation** (calculate, define, explain) : σ = sqrt[∑(x − μ)² / N] (or frequency form), measuring spread for a population.

# Section: seg_37 Heading：3.1 terminology
- **Probability** (define, calculate, interpret) : A measure of how certain we are of outcomes of a particular experiment or activity.
- **Experiment** (define, recognize, exemplify) : A planned operation carried out under controlled conditions.
- **Chance Experiment** (define, identify, exemplify) : An experiment whose result is not predetermined.
- **Outcome** (define, identify, list) : A result of an experiment.
- **Sample Space** (define, list, describe) : The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment; denoted by S and representable by a list, tree diagram, or Venn diagram.
- **Event** (define, identify, compute) : Any combination of outcomes, typically denoted by uppercase letters such as A or B.
- **Probability Range** (define, state) : Probabilities lie between zero and one, inclusive.
- **Equally Likely Outcomes** (define, recognize, calculate) : Each outcome of an experiment occurs with equal probability.
- **Probability For Equally Likely Outcomes** (compute, identify, list) : P(A) is computed as the number of outcomes in event A divided by the total number of outcomes in the sample space.
- **Long-Term Relative Frequency** (define, describe, interpret) : The probability of an outcome interpreted as its long-run relative frequency.
- **Law Of Large Numbers** (define, describe, state) : With increasing repetitions of an experiment, the observed relative frequency approaches the theoretical probability.
- **Biased (Unfair) Outcomes** (define, contrast, explain) : Situations in which outcomes are not equally likely due to bias in the device or process.
- **Or Event** (define, identify, compute) : The event containing outcomes in A or in B or in both A and B.
- **And Event** (define, identify, compute) : The event containing outcomes that are in both A and B at the same time.
- **Complement Of An Event** (define, list, calculate) : The set of all outcomes not in event A; denoted A′.
- **Complement Rule** (state, compute, identify) : P(A) + P(A′) = 1.
- **Conditional Probability** (define, calculate, identify) : The probability of A given B, written P(A|B), calculated as P(A AND B) divided by P(B) where P(B) > 0; the condition reduces the sample space to B.

# Section: seg_39 Heading：3.2 independent and mutually exclusive events
- **Independent Events** (define, compute, justify) : Two events for which P(A|B) = P(A), P(B|A) = P(B), or P(A AND B) = P(A)P(B); knowing one occurs does not affect the chance the other occurs.
- **Dependent Events** (define, classify, justify) : Two events that are not independent; the occurrence of one affects the probability of the other.
- **Sampling With Replacement** (define, classify, calculate) : A sampling method where each selected member is replaced before the next pick, allowing members to be chosen more than once and making selections independent.
- **Sampling Without Replacement** (define, identify, explain) : A sampling method where selected members are not replaced, so each can be chosen only once and later probabilities are affected, making selections dependent.
- **Mutually Exclusive Events** (define, identify, justify) : Events that cannot occur at the same time; they share no outcomes and satisfy P(A AND B) = 0.

- **Dependent Variable** (define, identify, explain) : The variable y whose values depend on the independent variable x.
- **Slope** (calculate, interpret, define) : The coefficient b in y = a + bx that represents the rate of change in y for a one-unit increase in x.
- **Y-Intercept** (calculate, interpret, identify) : The constant a in y = a + bx; the value of y when x = 0 and the point where the line crosses the y-axis.
- **Scatter Plot** (construct, asses, interpret) : A graph used to assess the direction and strength of the relationship between x and y and evaluate linearity.
- **Regression Line (Line Of Best Fit)** (calculate, construct, use) : A line drawn on a scatter plot to model the relationship and make predictions between x and y.
- **Least-Squares Regression Line** (calculate, construct, interpret) : The regression line that minimizes the Sum of Squared Errors, providing a uniform best fit.
- **Residuals** (define, calculate, interpret) : The differences between observed y values and the corresponding estimated y values from the regression line.
- **Sum Of Squared Errors** (define, explain, use) : The sum of squared residuals; its minimum identifies the least-squares regression line.
- **Coefficient Of Correlation** (calculate, interpret, asses) : Pearson’s r, a measure of the strength and direction of linear association between x and y, bounded between −1 and +1.
- **Coefficient Of Determination** (define, interpret, explain) : r², the proportion (often expressed as a percent) of variation in y explained by x via the regression line.
- **Standard Deviation Of Residuals** (calculate, use, detect) : The statistic s used to estimate the population standard deviation of y based on the residuals.
- **Significance Testing Of Correlation Coefficient** (state, asses, explain) : A linear regression t-test of H0: ρ = hypothesized value (commonly 0) to assess whether a linear relationship exists in the population.
- **Prediction Using Regression** (use, calculate, explain) : Using the least-squares regression line to predict y after establishing a strong correlation.
- **Outlier** (define, identify, explain) : An observation that does not fit the pattern of the rest of the data.
- **Extrapolation** (identify, explain, define) : Making predictions for x-values outside the observed data range using the regression line, which should be avoided.

# Section: seg_179 Heading：13.1 one-way anova
- **One-Way ANOVA** (define, list, state) : A test used to determine the existence of a statistically significant difference among several group means, using variances to assess whether the means are equal.
- **Assumptions Of One-Way ANOVA** (list, state, define) : Conditions required to perform the test: populations are normal; samples are randomly selected and independent; populations have equal standard deviations (or variances); the factor is a categorical variable; the response is a numerical variable.
- **Null Hypothesis (One-Way ANOVA)** (define, state, compare) : All group population means are equal (H0: μ1 = μ2 = ... = μk).
- **Alternative Hypothesis (One-Way ANOVA)** (define, state, contrast) : At least two group population means are not equal (μi ≠ μj for some i ≠ j).
- **Factor** (define, differentiate, recognize) : A categorical variable.
- **Response** (define, identify, classify) : A numerical variable.

# Section: seg_181 Heading：13.2 the f distribution and the f-ratio
- **F Distribution** (define, describe, calculate) : Distribution used for F-tests with separate numerator and denominator degrees of freedom; derived from the Student’s t-distribution with values equal to squared t-values; denoted F ~ Fdf(num),df(denom).
- **F Statistic (F-Ratio)** (define, calculate, interpret) : Ratio of mean square between groups to mean square within groups, F = MSbetween/MSwithin; approximately 1 under the null hypothesis and generally larger than 1 when the null is false.
- **One-Way ANOVA** (calculate, construct, state) : Procedure that extends the t-test to compare more than two groups; preferred over multiple pairwise t-tests to avoid increasing Type I error; the hypothesis test is always right-tailed.
- **Null Hypothesis (One-Way ANOVA)** (define, state, explain) : All group population means are equal.
- **Alternative Hypothesis (One-Way ANOVA)** (define, state, recognize) : At least two sample groups come from populations with different normal distributions.
- **Variance Between Samples (Between-Groups Variation)** (define, calculate, describe) : An estimate of σ² given by the variance of sample means multiplied by n when group sizes are equal (weighted otherwise); also called variation due to treatment or explained variation.
- **Variance Within Samples (Within-Groups Variation)** (define, calculate, describe) : An estimate of σ² given by the average of the sample variances (pooled variance; weighted when group sizes differ); also called variation due to error or unexplained variation.
- **Sum Of Squares Between (SSbetween)** (define, describe, calculate) : Sum of squares representing variation among different samples; SSbetween = Σ[(s_j)²/n_j] − (Σ s_j)²/n.
- **Sum Of Squares Within (SSwithin)** (define, calculate) : Sum of squares representing variation within samples due to chance; SSwithin = SStotal − SSbetween.
- **Total Sum Of Squares (SStotal)** (define, calculate) : Sum of squares of all values combined minus the square of the total sum divided by n.
- **Mean Square (MS)** (define, calculate, use) : Mean square (variance estimate); MSbetween = SSbetween/dfbetween and MSwithin = SSwithin/dfwithin.
- **Degrees Of Freedom Between (dfbetween)** (state, calculate, identify) : dfbetween = k − 1.
- **Degrees Of Freedom Within (dfwithin)** (define, calculate, identify) : dfwithin = n − k.

# Section: seg_185 Heading：13.4 test of two variances
- **F Test Of Two Variances** (calculate, use, compare) : A procedure using the F distribution to compare two population variances.
- **Assumptions For Two-Variance F Test** (state, identify, explain) : The two populations are normally distributed and independent.
- **F Statistic For Two Variances** (define, calculate, interpret) : F = (s1^2/σ1^2) / (s2^2/σ2^2); under H0: σ1^2 = σ2^2, F = s1^2 / s2^2.
- **F Distribution And Degrees Of Freedom** (state, calculate, use) : F ~ F(n1 − 1, n2 − 1) with numerator degrees of freedom n1 − 1 and denominator degrees of freedom n2 − 1.
- **Hypotheses And Tail Direction** (state, classify) : H0: σ1^2 = σ2^2; the alternative may be less than, greater than, or not equal, leading to left-, right-, or two-tailed tests.
- **Orientation Of F Ratio** (recognize, explain, calculate) : The ratio may be s1^2/s2^2 or s2^2/s1^2, depending on the alternative hypothesis and which sample variance is larger.
- **Interpretation Of F Magnitude** (interpret, compare, explain) : F close to one supports equal variances; a much larger F (with the larger sample variance in the numerator) indicates evidence against equality.
- **Sensitivity To Non-Normality** (state, describe, explain) : The test is very sensitive to deviations from normality and can yield unpredictable p-values when distributions are not normal.

# Section: seg_187 Heading：13.5 lab: one-way anova
- **Analysis Of Variance (ANOVA)** (state, compute, justify) : A method of testing whether the means of three or more populations are equal; applicable when populations are normally distributed, have equal standard deviations, and samples are randomly and independently selected; the test statistic is the F-ratio.
- **One-Way ANOVA** (state, compute, interpret) : A method of testing whether the means of three or more populations are equal when there is one independent variable and one dependent variable; applicable under normality, equal standard deviations, and random, independent sampling; the test statistic is the F-ratio.
- **Variance** (define, calculate) : The mean of the squared deviations from the mean; the square of the standard deviation; the sample variance equals the sum of squared deviations divided by the sample size minus one.
- **F Distribution** (describe, state, interpret) : The distribution used for ANOVA and F tests, characterized by numerator and denominator degrees of freedom; it is always positive and skewed right.
- **F Statistic (F-Ratio)** (define, calculate, interpret) : The ratio of a measure of variation among group means to a similar measure of variation within groups; used as the test statistic in ANOVA.
- **Assumptions For One-Way ANOVA** (list, state, asses) : Each population is normal; samples are randomly selected and independent; populations have equal standard deviations (or variances).
- **Hypotheses In One-Way ANOVA** (state, define) : Null hypothesis states that all group means are equal; alternative hypothesis states that one or more group means differ.
- **Degrees Of Freedom (F Distribution)** (compute, state, describe) : Numerator degrees of freedom equal the number of groups minus one; denominator degrees of freedom equal the number of observations minus the number of groups.
- **Sum Of Squares Between (SSbetween)** (compute, use, identify) : The sum of squares measuring variation among group means (factor/between-groups variation).
- **Sum Of Squares Within (SSwithin)** (compute, state, define) : The sum of squares measuring variation within groups; equal to total sum of squares minus between-groups sum of squares.
- **Total Sum Of Squares (SStotal)** (compute, use) : The total variation across all observations.
- **Mean Square Between (MSbetween)** (compute, use) : The between-groups sum of squares divided by its degrees of freedom.
- **Mean Square Within (MSwithin)** (define, compute, use) : The within-groups sum of squares divided by its degrees of freedom.
- **Test Of Two Variances** (state, compute, asses) : An F test to determine whether two population variances are equal; uses the F distribution with numerator and denominator degrees of freedom one less than the corresponding sample sizes; assumes normal distributions and independence between populations.
- **Pooled Variance** (define, calculate, use) : The mean of the sample variances.
